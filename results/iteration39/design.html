HOW THE DUEL WORKS

The market moves first

A 14×10 board hangs over the live price feed. Every 2.2 seconds a candle closes and the market is forced to play: it drops a crimson stone into every price-row its candle range touched in that column. Calm tape prints thin walls; volatile tape prints thick ones. The chart isn't decoration — it is your opponent's hand, and reading it is the whole game.

You answer with 9 stones

Between candles, click any empty cell in the columns still ahead of the live one to pre-place a cyan stone — only 9 for the whole round, so every skip is a decision. Sandwich a line of market stones between two of yours (any row, column or diagonal) and you flank-capture the whole run: it flips gold and joins your side — whether you close the trap yourself or the tape prints into an ambush you set in advance. But the market captures back: if its prints bracket your stone, it's gone.

Settlement at the bell

When column 14 closes, your longest connected chain (cyan + captured gold, diagonals count) sets the multiplier — from 0.25× at chain 2 up to 10× at chain 8+ — plus 0.30× for every stone you flipped. One rule keeps you honest: a stone only counts if it's engaged — touching a crimson stone at the bell (captured golds always count). Hiding in empty corners scores zero; you must fight where the tape fights, which is exactly where it can flank you back.

# CAUSTIC — the market is your mirror

**One-line pitch:** Aim three light cannons down at a live price chart whose curve is a
liquid-mercury mirror; your beams reflect off the evolving path (multi-bounce), and at the
bell you're paid by which odds-zones on the frame your reflected rays are illuminating.

## Skin-free core sentence

> The player's bet is **the committed position + angle of a small number of fixed ray
> emitters aimed at the chart**, and it's settled by **which fixed-odds boundary zones the
> rays terminate in after deterministic specular (multi-bounce) reflection off the realized
> price curve at the bell** — plus a convergence bonus when several rays land in one zone.

## Core loop (second to second)
1. **AIM (6 s):** the chart streams live. Drag each of your 3 cannons along the top rail
   (position) and drag its diamond handle to set the beam angle. A dashed forecast shows
   where each beam *would* land off the current curve. Set stake. The bell timer arms.
2. **FLIGHT (16 s):** beams ignite. Every tick the market redraws the mirror under your
   rays: strike points slide, reflected rays sweep across the zone rail, projected payout
   ticks up/down live. A steep candle under a beam whips its reflection across half the
   board — every tick matters until the bell because the mirror keeps deforming.
3. **BELL:** the frame freezes, rays flash white, each landing zone bursts, payout = sum of
   (stake/3 × zone multiplier), ×1.5 if two rays converge in one zone, ×3 "CAUSTIC FOCUS"
   if all three converge. One top zone each round is the roaming ×8 PRIME zone.
4. 3 s settle card → next AIM. Auto-replays your last aim/stake if you idle (demo attract
   mode keeps the hero alive).

## How the live market drives it
The price polyline IS the physics object: each segment's slope is a mirror facet. Level
decides *where* on the ray the strike happens; slope decides the reflected direction;
curvature (concavity) decides whether a ray double-bounces and escapes sideways into the
high-odds rails. Calm chop = predictable near-vertical returns into cheap center zones;
trends tilt every facet and push whole fans of light sideways; vol bursts scatter rays into
corners and voids. Reading regime (drift, vol, mean-reversion) is exactly the skill that
prices your aim.

## Bet & payout
- Stake split equally across the 3 beams.
- Zone board: center-top zones ×1.2–×1.4 (easy, flat-market returns), shoulders ×2, top
  rims ×4, upper side rails ×3 (west) / ×5 (east), roaming PRIME ×8. Lower side walls and
  the floor (shots past the mirror's frontier) are 0× VOID — a ray flattened by a steep
  trend dies there; real risk for greedy shallow angles.
- Convergence: 2 rays in one zone ×1.5 on those rays; all 3 → ×3 on everything.

## Why it's addictive
The reflection operator amplifies price motion: a 1-pixel slope change at the strike point
swings the landing spot by hundreds of pixels at the rail — so the last seconds before the
bell are a lightshow of rays sweeping past the ×8 PRIME, kissing it, leaving it. "It was IN
the eight for half a second" is the one-more-round engine. Aim mastery is real (shallow
angles travel further but risk VOID; aiming at the frontier is volatile, aiming at old path
is suicide because it scrolls away), so regulars develop styles: tight triple-stack on
center for steady ×1.2s with convergence ×3, or wide corner-hunting fans.

## Why it fits Deriv
It's a fixed-odds, fixed-expiry bet on what the *shape* of the market will be at the bell —
level, slope and curvature, the same greeks a trader reads — wrapped in instant visual
feedback on a live chart. Stake → 22-second round → settle → restake: Deriv cadence.

## What makes it original
Every kept game settles on something the price itself does (touch, dwell, travel, rank,
extremum, containment). CAUSTIC inserts a deterministic *geometric transform* between the
path and the payout: light reflecting off the curve. The player doesn't predict the path —
they aim through it, reasoning about how an unknown future surface will bend a known ray.
No trading game found (see originality.md) uses the price curve as a specular mirror.
